# Hairline needs craft layers, not more charts

Hairline is already a very broad kit, but it is not yet a comprehensive one in the sense the leading taxonomies mean. About 90 gallery charts cover roughly 35 of the 60 or so forms in the FT Visual Vocabulary and the Data Viz Catalogue, usually under bespoke names (RungBars, DotCascade, TickBox), and about 25 forms are genuinely absent: arrow plot, bullet, error bar and fan band, population pyramid, plain bump, connected scatter, funnel, marimekko, Gantt, radar, sunburst, horizon, hexbin, and every map except two choropleths. The bigger shortfall is cross-cutting. Hairline has no reusable reference line or band, no annotation layer, no facet wrapper, no uncertainty marks, no log or sqrt axis, and no shared size-and-colour legend, and those would improve the existing charts more than any new form. The finishing details worth stealing are small and mostly structural: a paper-coloured halo on every label, end-of-line direct labels with a collapsed group label, a hero-versus-context switch in both grey and opacity modes, the Economist's translucent-context and opaque-highlight dots, ridgelines drawn back to front with opaque fills, an exemplar-glyph legend borrowed from Stefaner and Lupi, and a pointer model that snaps by x with a reach limit and a flip-only-when-clipped tooltip. Glow, gooey filters, hue ramps and organic blobs clash with the style and should stay out. The ranked list at the end points each detail at specific charts. Treat the evidence as uneven: the newsroom and studio notes are strongest on labels, annotation and Bremer's SVG techniques, and thin or empty on slopegraph end labels, fan-chart specs, scrub readouts at the NYT or FT, and every proprietary product (Stripe, Linear, Vercel, Plausible), where nothing was retrieved.

## Roughly 35 of 60 forms are covered, and the 25 gaps are mostly cheap variants

Measured against the union of the FT Visual Vocabulary, the Data Viz Catalogue, Datawrapper, Observable Plot, Vega-Lite, ECharts, Nivo, visx and RAWGraphs, the unified list runs to about 110 forms that collapse into roughly 15 families ([FT Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/blob/main/visual-vocabulary/README.md); [Data Viz Catalogue](https://datavizcatalogue.com/); [Plot marks](https://github.com/observablehq/plot/tree/main/docs/marks); [ECharts index](https://echarts.apache.org/en/llms.txt); [nivo.rocks](https://nivo.rocks/)). Hairline's local inventory maps well onto the editorial core. Bars, paired and stacked bars, diverging bar, lollipop, dumbbell, slope, line, area, stacked area, streamgraph, area difference, candlestick, waterfall, histogram, box, violin, ridgeline, strip, scatter, bubble, four heatmap idioms, calendar, treemap, circle pack, tree, five waffle-family variants, parallel coordinates, Sankey, chord and several network forms all exist, though under idiosyncratic names. The earlier report found that only six charts have zod schemas and subpaths, so "covered" in the gallery overstates what an agent or app consumer can reach through a typed contract. The coverage audit also did not open each chart's props, so questions such as whether JitterStrip packs like a beeswarm or Candlestick has an OHLC mode remain open.

The absent set splits into three groups. The first is forms that reuse geometry Hairline already owns. An arrow plot is a one-prop variant of DumbbellQueue, and Datawrapper made arrow and range plots first-class types and then added annotations to seven chart types, which is a decent signal of newsroom demand ([Datawrapper arrow and range plots](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/new-arrow-and-range-plots); [annotations](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/annotations-in-bar-charts)). A bullet chart is TickGauge with a target tick. A population pyramid or diverging stacked bar is DivergingBar mirrored. A fan chart is AreaDifference with bands. A plain bump chart is a line chart with curved lines, which Datawrapper recommends precisely because the curve helps the eye follow a series ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/favorite-popular-chart-types)). Hairline today has bump behaviour only in FeatureRank, RankStrip and the animated BarRace. The second group is forms that appear in three or more independent sources and so belong in anything that calls itself comprehensive: marimekko (Catalogue, Flourish, Nivo), sunburst or icicle (Catalogue, ECharts, Nivo, RAWGraphs), hexbin (Plot, RAWGraphs), horizon (RAWGraphs), Gantt (Catalogue, RAWGraphs) and funnel (ECharts). The third group is costly and niche: flow maps, cartograms, proportional-symbol maps, Venn, word cloud, kagi, point and figure, and 3D. Radar is the one gap the FT itself lists and Hairline should probably skip, on the editorial grounds the notes flag as an inference and not a sourced position.

The practical ranking is by frequency in newsroom taxonomies and by how thin a variant Hairline can build. No source gives usage frequency for chart types, so the ordering is judgement. Small multiples, reference lines and uncertainty marks come first because they are capabilities that lift twenty charts at once, then arrow, bullet, pyramid, bump and connected scatter, then funnel, marimekko and Gantt, and only then maps and the long tail. For monochrome engraving, horizon is an unusually good fit, because a stack of hatch-density bands is the same ladder Hairline already uses for magnitude, even though it is niche.

## Six cross-cutting capabilities outrank any new chart form

A grep of the repo found no reusable annotation, reference-line, facet, uncertainty or axis-scale layer. Season bands exist inside board TimeLines, plan and limit lines are baked into two board presets, SparkGrid is a fixed 3x3 shared-cursor grid, and log scale appears in exactly one chart (RadialPatchwork). The layout `scale` prop in the schemas is a size control, not an axis scale. The peers treat these as core: Plot ships faceting, `rule`, `tip`, `difference`, `bollinger` and `linear-regression`, Vega-Lite ships `errorbar` and `errorband` composite marks, and visx ships separate annotation, brush, legend and threshold packages ([Vega-Lite marks](https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/docs/mark.html); [visx packages](https://github.com/airbnb/visx/blob/master/packages/visx-visx/src/index.ts)). A caveat applies: the six schema-backed charts were only grepped, so one of them may already carry a reference prop.

The annotation layer is a design decision before it is a feature, because Hairline's README says charts draw only the plot and carry no prose. The earlier work on an opinionated library resolved the tension by putting annotation in its own optional, serialisable layer that is off in the gallery, and the notes here supply the specifics. Datawrapper measures annotation width in chart-relative units so text re-wraps when the chart shrinks, and on small screens it numbers the annotations and moves their text below the chart ([Datawrapper responsive annotations](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/better-more-responsive-annotations-in-datawrapper-data-visualizations)). That numbered-marker fallback keeps the plot free of paragraphs at narrow widths, which suits Hairline's rule. visx splits an annotation into subject, connector and label with `dx`/`dy` offsets, which gives a clean prop model for point notes, ranges and leaders ([@visx/annotation](https://npmjs.com/package/@visx/annotation)). Swift Charts adds the missing edge policy: overflow is chosen per axis, so a label may slide horizontally to fit the plot while remaining free vertically, or the scale may grow to make room (`padScale`) ([Apple docs](<https://developer.apple.com/documentation/charts/chartcontent/annotation(position:alignment:spacing:overflowresolution:content:)-1kiow.md>)). Datawrapper's rule for the text itself is two tiers only, such as 12 px grey and 14 px black, left- or right-aligned, never shrunk to fit ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/text-in-data-visualizations)). Hairline's existing type roles can carry both tiers.

Uncertainty needs a stated-coverage rule more than a band. The NHC cone is sized to capture about 67% of recent forecast errors, yet readers treat the edge as the limit, which is why a band's probability coverage should be a required label ([Skeptical Science summary](https://skepticalscience.com/hurricane-cone-uncertainty-graphics.html), a secondary source). For discrete outcomes, research on election forecasts supports quantile dotplots of about 20 to 100 dots, and Wilke warns that too many dots read as a continuum ([Wilke](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/visualizing-uncertainty.html), via search snippet). The NYT election needle is the instructive failure. Its jitter ran across the 25th to 75th percentile of a couple of thousand simulated outcomes to convey uncertainty, and Quartz argued that readers found trends in noise from an update that arrived about every 30 seconds ([Visualising Data](https://visualisingdata.com/2016/11/gauging-election-reaction/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/831944/the-new-york-times-election-result-projections-are-mostly-random-jitters)). Hairline's TickGauge should never animate noise. If it shows uncertainty, it should draw the interval as ticks and offer a verbal rendering alongside.

## Newsroom details that cost almost nothing and show on every chart

The most reproducible newsroom habits are the humblest. Datawrapper's analysis of NYT, Bloomberg and WaPo work recommends removing the key and labelling directly, and giving any text that sits over marks or even a gridline a halo, a stroke "most often in the background color of your chart" ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/text-in-data-visualizations)). Because Hairline's paper is a defined token, a `paint-order: stroke` halo in paper colour is a default for every SVG text element, with no per-chart decision. The same trick separates same-colour lines where they cross: NYT's May 2021 vaccine-inequality chart gives every line a white stroke ([Datawrapper, 10 ways to use fewer colors](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/10-ways-to-use-fewer-colors-in-your-data-visualizations)). The NYT's February 2022 covid-deaths chart shows the matching label rule: all lines in one colour, direct labels on the right, and the crowded left side collapsed into a single group label, "Other large, high-income countries" (same source). That is a concrete API for HairlineLine and board TimeLines: end labels with collision nudging, plus a `group` that merges the losers.

Highlight needs two modes, because newsrooms split. The Economist and the NYT covid chart use lighter greys for context, while Bloomberg's Covid Resilience Ranking fades by opacity and labels only a few countries, leaving tooltips for the rest (same Datawrapper source). Datawrapper cautions that faded series can vanish unless the palette is darkened, and that translucent symbols want a fully opaque outline ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/colors-for-data-vis-style-guides)). The Economist's 2017 style guide supplies the scatter spec in numbers: a standard dot is 4 px at 50% opacity, a highlight dot is 4 px at 100%, a category dot has a 0.3 px stroke and 50% fill, and an index marker is a 5 pt black circle with a 0.5 pt stroke on a 0.5 pt red line ([Economist style guide](https://design-system.economist.com/documents/CHARTstyleguide_20170505.pdf)). The notes say the PDF was read through jumbled text extraction, so those figures should be checked against the PDF before they become tokens. Burn-Murdoch's excess-deaths chart supplies the motion version: add earlier years one by one, fade them to a near-white grey (`#ececec` in Flourish's rebuild), and let the outlier enter in one saturated colour ([Flourish](https://flourish.studio/blog/masters-reveal-line-john-burn-murdoch/)). In a one-ink house style the equivalent is hero ink at full weight against `--l` ladder greys, with the opacity mode available when the ladder runs out.

Form-specific newsroom detail is sparse, and the notes say so plainly. The best-documented item is the ridgeline. Datawrapper's version masks each row against a running maximum because its tool lacks z-ordered fills, and the notes infer that a React SVG library can simply paint rows back to front with an opaque paper fill and a 1 px ink stroke ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/weekly-ridgeline-plot/)). Ridgeline's overlap parameter then plays the role of Datawrapper's gap. The NYT treemap of the November 2021 reconciliation bill uses three white separations (thin round unlabelled parts, thicker round big categories, a wide gap round one section), which is a ready hierarchy of gutters for NestedTreemap ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/10-ways-to-use-fewer-colors-in-your-data-visualizations)). Datawrapper warns that a bump chart's ranks hide magnitude, since locator maps kept the same rank while use rose 60%, so symbol size or a tooltip value should carry the underlying quantity (same bump source). Reuters' US cartogram offers a rectangular grid layout for mobile, and the ONS beeswarm template notes that narrow widths make the swarm grow taller and spill out of the SVG ([Reuters awesome-charts](https://github.com/reuters-graphics/awesome-charts); [ONS beeswarm](https://github.com/ONSdigital/beeswarm), snippet only). Hairline's JitterStrip needs a height-aware layout for the same reason. The Pudding's `checkOverlap.js` action, which flags overlapping labels instead of solving them, is a lighter alternative to a force solver for dot labels ([svelte-starter](https://github.com/the-pudding/svelte-starter)).

## Studio craft survives in monochrome if it is structural, not atmospheric

The studio notes are explicit about what to take and what to leave. The strongest reusable idea is the legend drawn as one exemplar glyph. Stefaner's OECD Better Life Index legend is a single labelled flower with its 11 petals named ([Truth & Beauty](https://truth-and-beauty.net/projects/oecd-better-life-index)), and Dear Data postcards carry a hand-drawn key on the back that makes every symbol decodable ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/dear-data/story); [Kairos](https://kairos.technorhetoric.net/25.1/praxis/sorapure-fauni/design.html)). Hairline's most custom charts, notably RadialPatchwork, BarcodeLollipop, BubbleAlmanac (size, core and dotted ring) and RadialYear, currently carry bespoke or no legends. A `GlyphKey` that renders one oversized instance of the mark with leader lines and axis-sized labels would make them readable at no cost to the restraint rule, and the "back of the card" variant, a flip or `<details>` panel with a sentence per symbol, is a good fit for the information popover.

Bremer's techniques are the best-documented recipes. A per-chord `linearGradient` with `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` and endpoints at the two arc midpoints (`x = r*cos(mid - PI/2)`) shows direction in ChordRing, and the notes suggest one ink at two opacities, say 0.9 to 0.15, instead of two hues ([Visual Cinnamon](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/06/orientation-gradient-d3-chord-diagram/)). The same gradient with two native `<animate>` elements on `x1`/`x2` over 7 s, symmetric stops and `spreadMethod="reflect"` imitates flow, a candidate for AggregateSankey and HourglassStream if used once per page and disabled under reduced motion ([Visual Cinnamon](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/05/animate-gradient-imitate-flow-d3/)). Royal Constellations contributes node opacity that fades with graph distance from a hero (almost transparent beyond six steps), large clipped voronoi cells under tiny nodes to enlarge hover targets, and one size jump for the 12 annotated leaders ([Data Sketches](https://www.datasketch.es/project/royal-constellations)). In one ink that becomes ForceGalaxy and ForceGraph with a hero node, BFS-distance opacity, and voronoi hit areas that JitterStrip, DottyMatrix and DotCascade need anyway. Reuters uses voronoi callbacks for spike-map tooltips as well ([Reuters](https://github.com/reuters-graphics/awesome-charts)). Olympic Feathers keeps a fixed arc length per medal so white gaps show missing history, and adds a single white dot for record-setting events ([Data Sketches](https://www.datasketch.es/project/olympic-feathers)). Both are one-line rules for RadialYear, ArcMatrix and CalendarHeat: never rescale to close a gap, and flag an exception with a small paper-coloured ring.

What clashes is just as documented. Bremer says of her glow filter that it is "definitely not something that will suit a business/no-nonsense chart" ([Visual Cinnamon](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/06/glow-filter-d3-visualization/)). Her gooey filter distorts sizes that encode data unless an `feBlend` on the source graphic preserves them ([Visual Cinnamon](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/2016/06/fun-data-visualizations-svg-gooey-effect/)). Fragapane, whose organic shapes carry human subjects, says they are not for dashboards ([Nightingale via search summary](https://medium.com/nightingale/federica-fragapane-on-visualizing-theatre-childrens-books-and-more-bcb9b5174b21)). A FlowingData commenter found OECD flower heights hard to compare ([FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2012/05/22/updated-oecd-better-life-index)). Hairline should therefore confine glow and gooey to the "big" and "original" showpieces, if anywhere, and keep hover effects to opacity and stroke width, not recolouring. Several construction tips in the studio notes, namely `textPath` for arc labels, seeded wobble under 0.5 px, hatch `<pattern>` angles, and `simulation.tick(300)` outside React with a seeded PRNG, are the author's general knowledge and not sourced, so verify them before building. The repo's own research suggests Hairline already has hatching and texture ids, so the open items are arc-following labels on RadialYear and ChordRing, where group names are still horizontal.

## Interaction details: one pointer model, one tooltip, one keyboard path

Product-level evidence is thinner than the brief hoped. Apple (Stocks, Weather, Swift Charts) and Robinhood were documented, and everything proprietary was not. What exists is consistent. Stocks and Robinhood scrub on touch-and-hold, which avoids hijacking page scroll, and Stocks adds a two-finger gesture for the difference over a period ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/check-stocks-iph1ac0b1bc/ios); [Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/gb/en/support/articles/using-advanced-charts/)). Apple's WWDC22 guidance stretches touch targets to the full plot height and demands keyboard, Voice Control and VoiceOver parity ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110340/)). The two-finger delta is a restrained alternative to brush zoom: two rules with a difference label, which suits PlumbScatter, HairlineLine and Slope better than a zoom control.

For the pointer model, Observable Plot is the most principled source. Its `pointer` transform ignores the pointer beyond a 40 px reach, `pointerX` divides the non-dominant axis distance by 100 so x dominates but y breaks ties on multi-series lines, click locks a tip so text can be copied, and pooled tips show at most one at a time ([Plot pointer docs](https://github.com/observablehq/plot/blob/main/docs/interactions/pointer.md)). visx supplies the flip rule: place the tooltip left only if right placement clips and clips more than left does, with a 10 px default offset, and keep the datum glyph on the data even when the crosshair does not snap ([TooltipWithBounds](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/airbnb/visx/master/packages/visx-tooltip/src/tooltips/TooltipWithBounds.tsx); [xychart Tooltip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/airbnb/visx/master/packages/visx-xychart/src/components/Tooltip.tsx)). Tremor's click-to-isolate dims others to 0.3 stroke opacity and adds invisible 12 px hit lines, which is exactly Hairline's `DimGroup` and `HitPath`, so the idea is already implemented and only the 12 px minimum needs checking ([Tremor LineChart](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tremorlabs/tremor/main/src/components/LineChart/LineChart.tsx)). shadcn's `line` and `dashed` tooltip indicators are better than filled swatches for line charts, since a swatch is a different mark from the series ([shadcn chart.tsx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shadcn-ui/ui/main/apps/v4/registry/new-york-v4/ui/chart.tsx)). Recharts 3's keyboard layer is the cautionary checklist: arrow keys that were not `preventDefault`ed scrolled the page in Safari, and a resting mouse beat the keyboard for tooltip priority ([issue 8047](https://github.com/recharts/recharts/issues/8047); [issue 7905](https://github.com/recharts/recharts/issues/7905)). Highcharts adds Home and End to jump to series ends, and WCAG 1.4.13 requires tooltips to be dismissible with Esc, hoverable and persistent ([Highcharts](https://www.highcharts.com/docs/sonification/navigation); [tooltip practice](https://github.com/mgifford/ACCESSIBILITY.md/blob/main/examples/TOOLTIP_ACCESSIBILITY_BEST_PRACTICES.md), a secondary source).

Motion details are mostly to avoid. NumberFlow's defaults are a 900 ms spring and a 450 ms ease-out fade, with `respectMotionPreference` turning animation off entirely, hidden tabs skipped, and `composite: 'accumulate'` so interruptions blend ([number-flow source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/barvian/number-flow/main/packages/number-flow/src/lite.ts)). Hairline's own tokens run 150 to 1200 ms with ease-in-out-quart, so a roll should be restricted to discrete changes such as a range switch, and scrub values should update instantly. AG Charts' model of one total duration split across remove, update and add phases is a clean way to describe choreography ([AG Charts](https://www.ag-grid.com/charts/react/animation/)). ECharts' `universalTransition` morphs between chart types by matching ids, but it needs stable keys and a shape-division strategy, so for Hairline it is a large optional extra and not a finish detail ([ECharts PR 15208](https://github.com/apache/echarts/pull/15208)). No source documented hysteresis, tooltip follow easing, skeletons or up/down value colouring for any product, so those would be original choices. For scroll-driven stories the Pudding's pattern is the sound one: `position: sticky` pins the graphic, the chart is a pure function of a step index, triggers fire at mid-viewport with pixel offsets on mobile, and a motion toggle is provided ([The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/process/scrollytelling-sticky/); [Scrollama](https://github.com/russellgoldenberg/scrollama)). That pure-step shape matches the `replayKey` and ready-signal design proposed earlier.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which charts is Hairline missing" to "which layers is it missing". The 25 absent forms are mostly thin variants of geometry the library already draws well, so the real gap is a shared language of reference marks, annotation, intervals, faceting and a pointer model that every chart inherits. Hairline's bespoke forms are its distinguishing asset and also its weakest point for readers, because nothing decodes them. The studio evidence, from Dear Data's back-of-card key to Stefaner's single labelled flower, says the legend is where unusual glyphs earn trust, which makes the exemplar-glyph key a higher priority than any additional chart.

Two judgements are open and should be settled by looking, not by more reading. The first is whether an annotation layer exists at all under the no-prose house rule, since the best newsroom and Datawrapper evidence treats annotation as the editorial core and the rule treats it as a breach. The second is how much of the craft shows at small sizes, since halos, hairline strokes and hatch pitches are all fragile at fractional scale, a risk the earlier report already raised. The evidence gaps matter too: no source documented slopegraph end labels, fan-chart band specs, scrub readouts at the major outlets, or any proprietary product's chart behaviour, so those details would be original design choices that Hairline can make and defend as its own.
